The Day the Lights Went Out

The power went out at 7:42 p.m. on a Tuesday, right in the middle of my math homework. I remember the exact time because the microwave clock blinked once and then died, like it was giving up on me too.

At first, everyone in the house reacted the way you would expect. My little brother screamed. My dad said a word he later pretended he had not said. My mom started opening drawers looking for candles we had not bought in years. I just sat at the kitchen table with my pencil still in my hand, staring at a problem about the volume of a cylinder that I suddenly could not see.

We found two candles, one of them shaped like the number 7 from my brother's birthday cake. We put them in the middle of the table and, because there was nothing else to do, we all sat down. No phones, because we were saving the batteries. No TV. No laptop humming in the corner. Just the four of us and a little wax seven slowly melting.

My dad started telling a story about the time the power went out when he was a kid in Pune, during the monsoon, and how his grandmother made everyone sing old film songs until the lights came back. Then he started singing one, very badly. My brother laughed so hard he snorted, which made my mom laugh, which made me laugh, and then we were all laughing at nothing in particular in the dark.

The lights came back at 9:15. The fridge shuddered back to life, the Wi-Fi router blinked its green eyes, and my brother immediately ran to the TV. Everything went back to normal so fast it was almost rude.

I still have not finished that cylinder problem. But I think about that night more than I think about most days with electricity. It is strange to realize that the brightest evening my family had that year happened in the dark.
